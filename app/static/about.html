What this is

This site scores football (soccer) transfers on a 0–100 scale, trained on real historical data. Two different things live here:

Historical scores (Browse and Loans pages) grade a transfer that already happened, using data from the player's entire time at the club: performance, market value change, playing time, and more. These are backward-looking reports, not predictions.

Predictions (the homepage and Compare page) estimate how a hypothetical transfer might go, using only information available before the move: age, recent form, fee, market value, and the clubs involved. Nothing about what actually happened afterward is used, since a real prediction can't see the future either.

What "success score" actually means

A historical transfer's score blends several ingredients: how well the player performed relative to their league's average, how their performance changed from before the move, how much their market value grew, how much they actually played, whether the fee looked reasonable in hindsight, and, where available, detailed FotMob stats (attacking output, defending, possession, overall rating). The exact weighting shifts by position: a striker's score leans heavily on goal involvement, a centre-back's leans on defending, and a goalkeeper's is judged almost entirely on shot-stopping and playing time, since goals scored mean nothing for a keeper.

Every score comes with a "why this score" breakdown showing the real underlying numbers. Click any card on the Browse or Loans page, or run a prediction, to see it.

How honest is the prediction model?

Predicting how a brand-new transfer will play out over years, from nothing but pre-transfer stats, is hard: injuries, tactics, and squad fit matter as much as the numbers a model can see. The homepage states the model's real, measured error rate on every prediction rather than presenting a single number as gospel, and shows a likely range based on similar historical transfers instead of a false-precision point estimate.

Where the data comes from

The underlying data is the transfermarkt-datasets project (~50k players, ~175k transfers, ~1.9M appearances), supplemented with detailed match stats pulled from FotMob for the leagues and seasons it covers. Only transfers with enough playing time on both sides of the move are scored, so this skews toward established first-team players rather than fringe squad moves.

The full technical writeup (every scoring decision, known limitation, and bug found along the way) is in the project README.
